# WeChat Article Growth

## Overview

Use this skill to turn fuzzy "write a good WeChat article" requests into a concrete article strategy.
Optimize for two related but different outcomes:

- `High read`: maximize open rate and recommendation potential.
- `High like`: maximize endorsement, resonance, saves, shares, and "在看"/点赞 intent.

Read [references/research-notes.md](references/research-notes.md) first when the task requires evidence-backed analysis.
Read [references/playbooks.md](references/playbooks.md) when the task requires titles, hooks, structures, or CTA patterns.

## Workflow

### 1. Define the actual objective

Classify the request before writing:

- `Reach-first`: campaign launch, account growth, hot topic reaction, search capture.
- `Endorsement-first`: brand trust, values alignment, conversion warming, community cohesion.
- `Balanced`: most公众号爆文 tasks. Optimize for both open and endorsement, but pick one primary metric.

State the primary metric explicitly in the output.

## 2. Diagnose the topic using the WeChat article lens

Score the idea on these dimensions before drafting:

- `Timeliness`: tied to current events, policy, industry shifts, or platform conversation.
- `Audience width`: understandable and relevant beyond a narrow niche.
- `Identity relevance`: lets readers signal taste, stance, competence, or care by sharing/liking.
- `Credibility`: grounded in firsthand experience, specific cases, or trusted sources.
- `Positive payoff`: offers clarity, relief, practical gain, or emotional recognition.
- `Discussability`: naturally invites comment, save, forward, or "我也这样想".

If the topic is weak on at least three dimensions, do not hide the weakness with ornamental writing. Reframe the angle first.

## 3. Separate the "open package" from the "endorsement package"

Design both packages explicitly.

For the `open package`, focus on:

- topical keyword;
- concrete payoff;
- a visible tension, contradiction, or curiosity gap;
- a title and first-screen hook that can survive feed scanning.

For the `endorsement package`, focus on:

- source credibility;
- emotional tone that is positive, constructive, or deeply understood;
- a viewpoint the reader is comfortable attaching their identity to;
- an ending that gives the reader a good reason to like, save, or share.

Do not assume the same sentence does both jobs equally well.

## 4. Build the article in the correct order

Draft in this order:

1. Audience and desired reader reaction.
2. Angle statement in one sentence.
3. Three title candidates with different emphasis.
4. First-screen hook.
5. Main structure.
6. Ending CTA.

Do not start from body paragraphs.

## 5. Write titles with bounded freedom

Prefer titles that combine two or more of these elements:

- hot topic or current trigger;
- number or quantified promise;
- specific person, role, organization, or scene;
- theme keyword the audience already recognizes.

Keep the title concrete. Avoid abstract value words with no scene, no stakes, and no object.

When providing title options, vary them by intent:

- `Searchable`: keyword-forward and explicit.
- `Emotional`: stronger resonance or stance.
- `Editorial`: cleaner and more premium.

## 6. Write the first screen to stop the scroll

The first screen should do one clear job within the first few lines:

- state the tension;
- surface an unexpected fact;
- present a vivid scene;
- ask a loaded question the target reader already cares about.

Delay background and exposition until after the reader understands why the article matters now.

## 7. Choose the right body structure

Use one of these default structures unless the task clearly needs another shape:

- `Hot-topic commentary`: event -> why it matters -> judgment -> action or implication.
- `Emotional resonance`: scene -> conflict -> insight -> takeaway.
- `Practical guide`: problem -> mistakes -> method -> checklist.
- `Narrative / non-fiction`: character -> turning point -> system cause -> reflection.

Match structure to topic. Do not force listicles onto narrative subjects.

## 8. Design the ending for likes and shares

Endings should convert agreement into action without begging.

Prefer CTA patterns such as:

- invite the reader to take a side on a real question;
- summarize one sentence worth forwarding to a friend;
- provide a compact checklist worth saving;
- name the reader's emotion or dilemma precisely enough that liking feels like endorsement.

Avoid generic closers like "欢迎点赞转发" unless the user explicitly wants a broad运营口播风格.

## 9. Return a useful deliverable

When the user asks for strategy or analysis, return:

- the inferred audience;
- the primary metric;
- the angle thesis;
- the commonality analysis;
- title options;
- first-screen hook;
- article outline;
- CTA recommendation;
- risks that could hurt read or like rate.

When the user asks for a full draft, still include a short preface with the chosen angle and why it should work.

## Guardrails

- Distinguish clearly between source-backed findings and your inference.
- Do not fabricate platform rules or recommendation secrets.
- Do not write "万能爆文公式". Explain why a pattern should work for this audience and this topic.
- Do not over-index on negative emotion for like-oriented content. Credibility and positive emotional payoff matter more.
- Do not add fallback filler paragraphs just to lengthen the article.
