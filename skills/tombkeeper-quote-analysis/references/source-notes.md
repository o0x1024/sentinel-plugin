## Theme map

### 1. Concept disambiguation

Recurring move:

- Take a common word or familiar pair of ideas.
- Show that everyday language hides multiple different logical relations.
- Use a stripped-down example to separate them.

Representative cases:

- A recent post on `是` splits one common verb into equality, subset, and goal-like relations, then shows how people exploit the ambiguity.
- Another post separates `立场 / 价值观 / 真理`, then tests them with time and universality.

What this suggests:

- tombkeeper often treats discourse errors as classification errors first.
- He prefers a clean conceptual partition before moral judgment.

### 2. Local-context correction

Recurring move:

- Take a discourse imported from the Anglosphere.
- Ask whether the Chinese social structure, labor structure, or historical setting actually matches it.
- Expose the mismatch with one concrete example.

Representative cases:

- A recent homepage post says some gendered labor discussion about Chinese dock work is actually describing U.S. labor conditions rather than Chinese migrant labor.
- A post on `American` jokes that many arguments begin with a translation problem rather than a real local problem.

What this suggests:

- A large part of the voice comes from forcing concepts back into local institutional reality.
- The rhetorical edge often comes from saying, in effect, "this problem belongs to somewhere else."

### 3. Technical demystification

Recurring move:

- Resist magical or apocalyptic framing around technology.
- Reduce the issue to infrastructure, data, incentives, hardware constraints, or information asymmetry.
- End with a colder market or engineering judgment.

Representative cases:

- The homepage includes posts on AI, security stocks, and war-time algorithmic targeting that repeatedly strip away mystique and turn the subject into data pipelines, compute, and accessible operational logic.
- Another post treats a future-energy discussion by asking a practical search question rather than arguing from hype.

What this suggests:

- The voice is technical without being techno-utopian.
- He often sounds like he is removing theatrical fog from a system description.

### 4. Practical social realism

Recurring move:

- Start from a small, physical, ordinary social behavior.
- Observe it clinically.
- Derive a surprisingly useful competence rule from it.

Representative cases:

- A 2019 post about handing out red envelopes turns eye contact and hand movement into a lesson about how low the baseline for social competence can be in technical workplaces.
- The move is not sentimental. It is observational, mildly amused, and utilitarian.

What this suggests:

- The posts often imply that everyday competence is rare because most people do not actually look carefully.

### 5. Hard-limit aphorisms

Recurring move:

- Compress a worldview into a short trade-off statement.
- Offer no comforting reconciliation.

Representative cases:

- A 2020 line on `活着 / 虚无主义 / 诚实` reduces existential posture to a three-way constraint.
- The effect comes from sharp incompatibility, not decoration.

What this suggests:

- The shorter aphorisms are usually compressed conclusions of a harsher worldview: reality contains trade-offs that cannot be talked away.

## Cross-cutting traits

### Reasoning habits

- Start from definition before attitude.
- Prefer examples that are concrete, domestic, or operational.
- Distrust imported slogans, especially when detached from institutions.
- Treat many viral arguments as frame errors rather than mere bad intentions.

### Tone habits

- Dry and unadorned.
- Sometimes mocking, but usually after the logic is already settled.
- More likely to sound impatient with confusion than emotionally expressive.

### Limits of the pattern

- Not every short post is a full philosophical statement.
- Some are just jokes, reposts, or topical remarks.
- Do not force every fragment into a grand coherent system.
